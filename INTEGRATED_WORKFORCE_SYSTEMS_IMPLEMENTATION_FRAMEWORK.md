# Integrated Workforce Systems: Implementation Framework and Toolkit for System Leaders

## Purpose of This Framework

This toolkit is designed for system leaders – those in Integrated Care Boards, chief executives, workforce directors, and partnership leaders – who are tasked with translating the strategic aspiration for integrated workforce systems into concrete changes in how work is organised and conducted across Leeds and West Yorkshire.

The framework provides:
- A structured approach to integration
- Decision-making tools for leaders
- Implementation roadmap with milestones
- Practical resources for each strategic priority
- Governance models for integration
- Change management approaches

---

## Part 1: Understanding Integration

### What Integrated Workforce Systems Look Like

**Integrated workforce systems enable:**

1. **Seamless workforce deployment** - Professionals move across organisational boundaries based on need and capability, not organisational affiliation
2. **Unified development pathways** - Training and development opportunities are available system-wide, not restricted by organisation
3. **Coordinated recruitment** - Hiring decisions are made with system-wide need in view, not just organisational need
4. **Shared accountability** - Workforce outcomes (recruitment, retention, development, diversity) are tracked and managed at system level
5. **Third sector integration** - Third sector workforce is embedded in planning and development, not treated as peripheral
6. **Flexible employment models** - Different employment arrangements (NHS employment, bank arrangements, third sector employment) are bridges, not barriers

### Why Integration Matters for Neighbourhood Healthcare

Neighbourhood healthcare systems depend on:
- **Cross-sector collaboration** - Professionals from different organisations working together to meet population health needs
- **Flexible workforce deployment** - Ability to move staff to where greatest need exists, not where organisational budgets allow
- **Shared understanding** - Common language, values, and approaches across sectors
- **Continuous improvement** - Learning shared across the system

None of this is possible without integrated workforce systems.

## Part 2: The Five-Step Implementation Roadmap

### Step 1: Establish Integration Leadership (Months 1-3)

**Purpose:** Create clear leadership for the integration agenda and build commitment from all partners.

**Key activities:**

1. **Establish Integration Steering Group**
   - Chair: Integrated Care Board workforce lead or equivalent
   - Membership: Chief executives or equivalent from all major NHS organisations, primary care representatives, third sector leads, HR/workforce directors
   - Frequency: Monthly (initially); monthly sustainable
   - Mandate: Drive forward integrated workforce strategy and overcome barriers

2. **Conduct Leadership Alignment Workshop** (Month 1)
   - Bring together all partners to:
     - Agree shared vision for integrated workforce systems
     - Identify barriers in current systems
     - Establish non-negotiable principles (see Part 3)
     - Agree communication approach to partners and colleagues
   - Output: Shared integration charter

3. **Communicate Integration Commitment**
   - Joint statement from all partner leaders
   - Communication to all colleagues about why integration matters
   - Communication to boards and governance bodies
   - Clear message: "Integration is not optional; it is essential for neighbourhood healthcare"

4. **Establish Integration Project Team**
   - Core team (0.5-1 FTE from each major partner)
   - Dedicated project management resource
   - Remit: Design and implement integrated systems
   - Resource: Dedicated budget for integration work (minimum £50-100k for year 1)

**Success measures:**
- ✓ Integration Steering Group established and meeting monthly
- ✓ Integration charter agreed by all partners
- ✓ Public commitment statements from all partner leaders
- ✓ Integration Project Team operational with clear remit

### Step 2: Design Integrated Workforce Planning System (Months 2-4)

**Purpose:** Create a unified approach to workforce planning that operates at system level while respecting organisational autonomy where appropriate.

**Key activities:**

1. **Map Current Workforce Planning Systems**
   - Conduct audit of current workforce planning approaches across all partners
   - Identify commonalities and differences
   - Document barriers to integration in current systems
   - Output: Workforce Systems Audit Report

2. **Design Integrated Workforce Planning Process**
   - Establish system-level workforce planning cycle
   - Define roles: What is planned at system level vs organisational level?
   - Create planning templates and processes
   - Establish governance for planning decisions
   - Output: Integrated Workforce Planning Handbook

   **Key design principles:**
   - System planning drives organisational planning (not vice versa)
   - All partners participate in planning regardless of size
   - Third sector needs are integrated from the start
   - Plan includes diversity, development, and retention – not just recruitment
   - Plan explicitly connects to neighbourhood health service model

3. **Establish System Workforce Metrics**
   - Agree on metrics to be tracked at system level:
     - Recruitment (number, diversity, time-to-hire)
     - Retention (turnover, time-in-role, career progression)
     - Development (training participation, qualifications achieved)
     - Diversity (representation by protected characteristics)
     - Satisfaction (engagement scores, turnover analysis)
   - Establish data collection processes
   - Create dashboard for monitoring
   - Output: System Workforce Metrics Framework

4. **Conduct First System Workforce Planning Cycle**
   - Apply integrated planning process to year 2 planning
   - Work through governance to resolve conflicts between organisational and system priorities
   - Make first set of joint planning decisions
   - Build credibility by making visible decisions that reflect integration

**Success measures:**
- ✓ Integrated Workforce Planning Handbook published
- ✓ System Workforce Metrics Framework established with baseline data
- ✓ First integrated planning cycle completed
- ✓ Organisational workforce plans aligned to system priorities
- ✓ Visible decisions made based on system (not organisational) priorities

### Step 3: Create Integrated Development and Recruitment Pathways (Months 3-6)

**Purpose:** Make it easy for colleagues to develop their careers and move between organisations. Make it easy for organisations to recruit from across the system.

**Key activities:**

1. **Design Integrated Recruitment System**
   - Establish system-wide recruitment standards (job descriptions, person specifications, interview questions)
   - Create shared job board accessible across system
   - Establish shared recruitment training for interviewers
   - Create standard offer terms and conditions for roles across partners
   - Output: Integrated Recruitment Handbook

   **Key features:**
   - Shared job descriptions that can be adapted by organisations
   - Central job board with cross-organisational visibility
   - Aligned salary scales where possible
   - Standardised offer terms and conditions
   - Fast-track pathways for internal candidates moving between organisations

2. **Establish Integrated Training and Development Pathways**
   - Map current training and development across all partners
   - Identify gaps and duplications
   - Design integrated pathways:
     - Shared induction for all new starters
     - Common development programmes (leadership, clinical skills, etc.)
     - Cross-organisational placement opportunities
     - Access to external training for all colleagues regardless of organisation
   - Output: Integrated Development Handbook

   **Key features:**
   - Shared induction programme delivered by all partners
   - Common leadership development pathway
   - Shared clinical skills training
   - Cross-organisational placements (minimum 3 months)
   - Funding for development allocated at system level
   - Transparent access to all development opportunities

3. **Create Career Pathway Framework**
   - Design role families showing progression routes
   - Include roles across all partners and sectors
   - Highlight cross-sector movement opportunities
   - Output: Integrated Career Pathways Guide

4. **Pilot Integrated Approaches**
   - Select 2-3 high-priority roles and pilot integrated recruitment
   - Evaluate pilot and refine approach
   - Expand to all roles based on learning
   - Build case studies of successful cross-organisational moves

**Success measures:**
- ✓ Integrated Recruitment Handbook published and implemented
- ✓ Integrated Development Handbook published
- ✓ Career Pathways Guide published and promoted
- ✓ Pilot recruitment processes completed
- ✓ First cohort of colleagues recruited through integrated process
- ✓ First cross-organisational moves completed

### Step 4: Embed Third Sector in System Workforce (Months 4-8)

**Purpose:** Move third sector from peripheral to core partner in workforce integration.

**Key activities:**

1. **Establish Third Sector Representation**
   - Ensure third sector representative on Integration Steering Group
   - Establish Third Sector Workforce Working Group
   - Include third sector in all workforce planning and design activities
   - Output: Third Sector Engagement Strategy

2. **Map Third Sector Workforce Needs and Opportunities**
   - Conduct audit of third sector workforce (size, skills, challenges, development needs)
   - Identify opportunities for integration (shared training, development, recruitment)
   - Identify barriers (funding, employment models, qualifications)
   - Output: Third Sector Workforce Analysis

3. **Design Third Sector Support Programmes**
   - Develop funding for third sector workforce development
   - Create shared training programmes accessible to third sector
   - Establish mentoring partnerships (NHS-third sector)
   - Create advancement pathways for third sector workers
   - Output: Third Sector Development Programme

4. **Integrate Third Sector into System Planning and Metrics**
   - Include third sector data in system workforce metrics
   - Include third sector needs in workforce planning
   - Track third sector representation in shared training
   - Report on third sector workforce outcomes
   - Output: Enhanced Workforce Metrics

5. **Create Employment Model Bridges**
   - Design pathways for third sector workers to access NHS employment
   - Design pathways for NHS workers to move to third sector (secondment, transfer)
   - Create shared staff bank for third sector workers
   - Explore social enterprise models that bridge NHS and third sector

**Success measures:**
- ✓ Third Sector Workforce Working Group established and meeting monthly
- ✓ Third Sector Workforce Analysis completed
- ✓ Third Sector Development Programme launched and funded
- ✓ Third sector represented in system metrics and planning
- ✓ First third sector colleagues participating in shared training
- ✓ First employment model bridging initiatives piloted

### Step 5: Establish Governance and Accountability (Months 6-12)

**Purpose:** Embed integrated workforce approaches in governance and accountability structures so they persist beyond initial implementation.

**Key activities:**

1. **Establish Integration Governance Structure**
   - Define decision-making authority for integration (what decisions require system-level agreement?)
   - Establish escalation routes for conflicts between organisational and system interests
   - Create accountability for integration outcomes
   - Output: Integration Governance Framework

   **Key governance features:**
   - Monthly Integration Steering Group
   - Quarterly Integration Board (system leadership reporting)
   - Annual Integration Review and refresh
   - Clear decision-making protocols
   - Conflict resolution procedures
   - Accountability for integration outcomes (in appraisals, performance management)

2. **Embed Integration in Strategy and Planning**
   - Integrate into Integrated Care Strategy
   - Integrate into each organisation's strategy
   - Integrate into annual planning cycles
   - Integrate into financial planning (shared investment in integration)
   - Output: Integration embedded in all strategies

3. **Establish Accountability for Integration Outcomes**
   - Include integration metrics in organisational performance frameworks
   - Include integration in executive appraisals
   - Report integration progress quarterly to boards
   - Create transparency about who is leading integration work
   - Output: Integration Accountability Framework

4. **Create Sustained Funding for Integration**
   - Establish dedicated budget for integration work
   - Fund Integration Project Team on sustained basis (not one-year project)
   - Fund shared training and development programmes
   - Fund third sector development support
   - Output: Multi-year Integration Budget

5. **Establish Communication and Engagement**
   - Create regular communication to all colleagues about integration progress
   - Establish feedback mechanisms for colleagues
   - Celebrate integration successes
   - Address concerns and barriers transparently

## Part 3: Decision-Making Frameworks for Leaders

### Framework 1: Should This Be Done at System Level or Organisational Level?

Use this framework when deciding whether a workforce practice should be standardised across the system or remain organisational decision.

**Criteria for SYSTEM-LEVEL Decision:**
- Affects ability to deliver neighbourhood healthcare
- Affects ability of colleagues to move across organisations
- Affects third sector integration
- Benefits from standardisation and scale
- Affects patient/service user experience
- Requires cross-organisational coordination

**Criteria for ORGANISATIONAL DECISION:**
- Specific to organisational context or model
- Affects internal operations only
- Does not impact cross-organisational working
- Organisations have different capability or maturity
- Local flexibility required for local needs

**Examples:**
- **System-level:** Recruitment standards, development pathways, salary scales, induction, leadership training, diversity targets
- **Organisational-level:** Internal structure, team size, specific internal training, local HR policies that don't affect cross-organisational working

### Framework 2: Addressing Resistance to Integration

When facing resistance to integration, use this framework to understand the concern and develop response.

| Type of Resistance | Likely Concern | Response Strategy |
|-------------------|----------------|------------------|
| **Organisational autonomy** | Fear of loss of control or identity | Clarify that integration at system level doesn't eliminate organisational identity. Local context still matters. |
| **Financial** | Fear of losing budget or paying for system partners | Agree on shared funding model. System investment benefits all. |
| **Clinical/Professional** | Fear that system needs will override professional judgment | System planning affects *what* we do; professional judgment remains for *how* we do it. |
| **HR/Operational** | Fear of increased complexity | Initial complexity decreases over time as systems embed. Short-term investment for long-term gain. |
| **Third sector specific** | Fear of being overwhelmed by NHS | Explicit commitment that third sector isn't subsumed. Dedicated support. Third sector-specific decision-making space. |

### Framework 3: Prioritising When Resources Are Limited

If you cannot do everything at once, use this framework to prioritise.

**Tier 1 (Do First):**
1. Establish Integration Steering Group and leadership alignment (foundation)

**Tier 2 (Do Quickly After):**

5. Embed Third Sector (essential for neighbourhood healthcare)

## Part 5: Addressing Common Implementation Challenges

### Challenge 1: "We've always done it this way organisationally. Why change?"

**Root cause:** Inertia, fear of change, unclear rationale for integration

**Response strategy:**
1. Make explicit case for why integration is essential for neighbourhood healthcare
2. Start with early wins in integrated recruitment and development
3. Celebrate colleagues who make cross-organisational moves
4. Share patient/service user benefit stories
5. Build integration into appraisals and accountability

### Challenge 2: "Integration will take too much time and resource."

**Root cause:** Legitimate concern about implementation burden

**Response strategy:**
1. Acknowledge legitimate implementation effort required
2. Show long-term efficiency gains (no more duplication, better coordination)
3. Structure as phased approach (start with highest value activities)
4. Provide dedicated project resource
5. Set realistic expectations for pace
6. Celebrate milestones

### Challenge 3: "Our third sector partners don't have capacity to engage."

**Root cause:** Third sector resource constraints, historically peripheral position

**Response strategy:**
1. Provide dedicated support for third sector engagement (funding, facilitation)
2. Design engagement to fit third sector capacity (don't require them to attend 5 meetings weekly)
3. Provide ring-fenced funding for third sector development
4. Ensure third sector voices heard in decision-making (don't just ask them to attend)
5. Recognise this as investment in integration, not burden on third sector

### Challenge 4: "We can't align salary scales – organisations have different funding models."

**Root cause:** Financial constraints in some partners (especially third sector)

**Response strategy:**
1. Acknowledge this is genuine challenge (don't dismiss it)
2. Start with non-salary alignment (role descriptions, development, career pathways)
3. Explore innovative solutions (system-wide levy, shared funding for roles)
4. Pilot integrated recruitment at similar salary levels, scale from there
5. Consider where salary differences are barriers vs where they're manageable
6. Recognise that alignment is ideal but not necessary for integration (bridges matter more than identical salaries)

### Challenge 5: "Organisational leaders won't agree to system-level decisions overriding organisational decisions."

**Root cause:** Legitimate concern about organisational autonomy

**Response strategy:**
1. Be clear about what integration requires (system-level recruitment and planning decisions)
2. Be clear about what integration doesn't require (eliminate organisational identity or internal structure)
3. Create governance where conflicts between system and organisational interests are explicitly resolved (not left hanging)
4. Show that system decisions ultimately benefit organisations (better workforce, better service)
5. Build trust through early implementation successes

## Part 6: The Integration Timeline and Expectations

### Year 1: Foundation and Early Implementation
- **Months 1-3:** Leadership alignment, steering group established, project team operational

- 50%+ of recruitment through integrated system
- Cross-organisational moves normalised
- System metrics baseline established
- Third sector workforce outcomes visible
- Integration embedded in annual planning

- Integration becomes business-as-usual
- Focus shifts from implementation to optimisation

**Expected outcomes:**
- 80%+ integration in recruitment
- Significant increase in cross-organisational moves
- Workforce composition reflecting system strategy
- Third sector fully integrated
- System-wide workforce identity alongside organisational identity

## Conclusion: Making Integration Real

This framework is structured around a fundamental truth: **integration requires deliberate, sustained effort across multiple dimensions.**

It will not happen by accident. It will not happen through good intentions. It will not happen through partnership statements alone.

Integration happens when:
- Clear leadership commits to integration
- Dedicated resource is devoted to integration
- Governance makes integration non-negotiable
- Colleagues understand why integration matters
- Early wins build confidence in integration
- Systems and processes embed integration
- Accountability ensures integration persists

This framework provides the structure. The success depends on your leadership, commitment, and willingness to do the work.

The reward is a workforce system genuinely integrated across primary, secondary, and third sectors. Colleagues who can move fluidly across boundaries. Neighbourhood healthcare supported by neighbourhood workforce.

That is worth the effort.
