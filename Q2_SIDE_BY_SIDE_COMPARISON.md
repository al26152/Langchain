## 📋 FINDING #1: Structural Barriers to Integrated Workforce

### MAIN BRANCH (Unfiltered)
```
**Governance and Structural Barriers to Workforce Collaboration**

[SYNTHESIZED] Leeds and York Partnership NHS Foundation Trust, like other
NHS trusts, faces significant governance and structural barriers that hinder
effective workforce collaboration. These include rigid workforce planning due
to national policy constraints, a lack of integrated workforce structures, and
bureaucratic barriers that prevent staff from fully utilizing their skills.

Supporting Evidence:
- [FACT] "The current workforce operates in isolated components and is
  imbalanced towards the acute sector – a trend which is on track to worsen."
  → NHS_Getting-Into-Shape_WEB-FINAL.md
- [FACT] "The current cap on medical training places introduces rigidity into
  workforce planning and undermines staff commitment to the service."
  → NHS_Getting-Into-Shape_WEB-FINAL.md
- [FACT] "The Trust remains challenged in terms of workforce, but this is
  also an opportunity to think about skills and roles, supported by a robust
  People Plan to underpin the work needed."
  → Leeds and York Partnership NHS Foundation Trust Annual-Report-and-Accounts-2024-25

Strategic Implication:
[INFERENCE] Overcoming these barriers will require Leeds and York Partnership
NHS Foundation Trust to advocate for policy changes at the national level and
invest in developing a more integrated and flexible workforce structure. This
includes revising workforce planning models and reducing bureaucratic
constraints.

Caution: National policy changes are outside Leeds and York Partnership NHS
Foundation Trust's direct control, and efforts may take considerable time to
manifest in tangible improvements.
```

### FEATURE BRANCH (Filtered)
```
**Finding 1: Structural Isolation of Workforce**

[SYNTHESIZED] The current NHS workforce operates in isolated components, which
is imbalanced towards the acute sector. This structural setup hinders the
development of an integrated workforce necessary for effective collaboration
across partnerships. This issue is compounded by bureaucratic barriers that
prevent staff from utilizing their full skill sets.

Supporting Evidence:
- [FACT] "Integrated care is not possible without an integrated workforce. Yet
  the current workforce operates in isolated components and is imbalanced
  towards the acute sector – a trend which is on track to worsen."
  → NHS_Getting-Into-Shape_WEB-FINAL.md

Strategic Implication:
[INFERENCE] Without addressing these structural barriers, efforts to achieve
integrated care through workforce collaboration may be significantly undermined.

Basis for inference:
- FACT: The workforce operates in isolated components.
- ASSUMPTION: Integration requires breaking down these components.
- LOGIC: An integrated workforce is essential for collaborative efforts; thus,
  structural isolation is a direct impediment.

Caution: This inference assumes that structural changes are feasible and that
current policies will allow for such transformations.
```

  - Main: Implies need for national policy advocacy
  - Feature: Implies need for structural redesign

[SYNTHESIZED] National policy reliance on volunteerism and the protection of

[INFERENCE] Governance frameworks need to be revised to encourage mandatory

collaboration.

- ASSUMPTION: Mandatory participation will aid integration.
- LOGIC: Cohesive governance is necessary to ensure all parties are aligned

Caution: This assumes that mandatory policies will be accepted and effective,
which may not account for resistance from independent entities.
