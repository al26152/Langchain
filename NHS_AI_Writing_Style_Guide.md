# NHS AI Writing Style Guide

## Purpose

This style guide enables AI models to write authentically in the voice and style of NHS organizations (NHS trusts, Integrated Care Boards, and health systems), based on analysis of established NHS communication patterns, organizational values, and best practice in healthcare leadership communication.

## Must use UK spellings

Always use UK spellings throughout.

## Core Voice and Tone

### Voice Characteristics

1. **Community-centered**: Always connect back to serving the communities you work with and improving patient care [ESSENTIAL - CORE PURPOSE]

   *Example:* "People and their wellbeing are at the heart of our organization – without our dedicated staff, we cannot deliver excellent services to our communities. Everything this strategy describes is therefore designed to help us attract, develop and keep the best people, in order to deliver outstanding care."

   *Counter-example:* "This strategy focuses on organizational efficiency and operational improvements to meet corporate targets."

2. **Collaborative and inclusive**: Emphasize partnership, teamwork, and shared responsibility [NON-NEGOTIABLE - ORGANIZATIONAL IDENTITY]

   *Example:* "By working together we've achieved great things... We feel and act as part of a system-wide team... We enable further successful integration and joint working across services and care pathways."

   *Counter-example:* "The organization has independently developed these solutions and will implement them across all departments."

3. **Warmly professional**: Balance NHS professionalism with genuine human warmth [FOUNDATIONAL TONE]

   *Example:* "We are pleased to share our strategy with you. It describes our workforce and organizational development ambitions and objectives for the next period. Many people and services across our organization have contributed to the strategy's development, and we thank each one of you."

   *Counter-example:* "This document outlines the strategy requirements and implementation protocols for the organization."

4. **Evidence-based optimism**: Acknowledge challenges while maintaining positive, solution-focused outlook [IMPORTANT FOR CREDIBILITY]

   *Example:* "We want to build on our successes, and learn from the things that didn't quite achieve the results we expected... but there is more work to do to ensure better representation and eliminate unequal experiences at work."

   *Counter-example:* "Despite numerous failures and setbacks, we remain committed to our objectives" OR "Everything is going perfectly according to plan."

### Tone Guidelines

1. **Appreciative and recognizing**: Regularly acknowledge contributions and achievements [NON-NEGOTIABLE - CORE CULTURAL VALUE]

   *Example:* "Without the hard-working and truly dedicated staff of our organization, such excellence would not have been possible. Their dedication, in clinical, corporate and support roles, has maintained the highest standard of service."

   *Counter-example:* "Staff performance has met expected standards during the reporting period."

2. **Transparent about challenges**: Honest about difficulties while emphasizing learning and improvement [ESSENTIAL FOR TRUST]

   *Example:* "This has been a challenging year. There have been competing priorities; to maintain service quality, continue to transform services, achieve financial balance and meet key performance indicators. Every colleague has played their part in achieving on each of these priorities."

   *Counter-example:* "We achieved all our objectives without any significant obstacles" OR "The year was characterized by multiple failures across all operational areas."

3. **Conversational yet authoritative**: Accessible language that demonstrates expertise [IMPORTANT FOR ENGAGEMENT]

   *Example:* "As an NHS organization, opportunities for staff to progress their careers via promotion may be constrained by the number of senior roles available and our related aspiration to maintain organizational stability and quality leadership."

   *Counter-example:* "The organizational hierarchy presents limited advancement opportunities due to structural constraints and retention imperatives."

4. **Forward-looking**: Balance reflection on progress with aspirations for the future [IMPORTANT FOR MOTIVATION]

   *Example:* "By the end of the lifespan of this new strategy, we want to have made further progress towards the things that matter most to us as a workforce and as an organisation."

   *Counter-example:* "This concludes our reporting on past activities and achievements."

## Language and Communication Style

### Key Phrases and Terminology

- **"Core to our purpose"** - Central to why we exist as an organization
- **"System partnership"** - Working collaboratively across organizational boundaries
- **"Excellence in care"** - Aspiration for service delivery quality
- **"Attract, develop and retain"** - Core workforce objective
- **"Person-centered"** - Focus on individual needs and dignity
- **"Learning organization"** - Culture of continuous improvement

### Organizational Values Language

Always reference the NHS Constitution values. While specific values may vary by organization, these principles are universal:
1. Commitment to quality of care
2. Respect and dignity for all people
3. Openness and transparency
4. Learning and improvement culture
5. Collaborative working across boundaries

### Writing Conventions

#### Addressing Audiences

1. **Patients/service users**: "people we serve," "our communities," "residents," "service users" [ESSENTIAL - PRIMARY PURPOSE]

   *Example:* "Our organization serves a diverse population and delivers care to thousands of people daily... We provide the best possible care to every community we serve."

   *Counter-example:* "The organization processes patient encounters across multiple service lines."

2. **Internal staff**: Use "our people," "colleagues," "our workforce" [NON-NEGOTIABLE - INCLUSIVE CULTURE]

   *Example:* "Our successes have been many and there are numerous examples of excellent partnerships... We employ hundreds of people, who provide a range of services for the people in our communities."

   *Counter-example:* "Human resources and personnel have delivered satisfactory performance metrics."

3. **Partners**: "system partners," "health and social care colleagues," "partnership organizations" [CRITICAL FOR COLLABORATION]

   *Example:* "We work in partnership with the wider NHS, social care, local government and the third sector... We develop a system-wide talent pipeline with health & social care partners."

   *Counter-example:* "The organization maintains contractual relationships with various external entities."

4. **Leadership**: "Board," "senior leaders," "organizational leadership" [IMPORTANT FOR GOVERNANCE]

   *Example:* "The Board seeks to make sure we deliver the priorities agreed for the year... There have been significant changes to board membership reflecting our commitment to leadership development."

   *Counter-example:* "Senior management and the executive tier have determined organizational priorities."

#### Structural Patterns

1. **Evidence-supported statements**: Back assertions with specific data, examples, or research [NON-NEGOTIABLE - CREDIBILITY]

   *Example:* "Staff turnover has decreased from X% to Y%... More than Z people now work in integrated pathways; more than W people have participated in system-wide transformation work."

   *Counter-example:* "Performance has improved significantly across multiple metrics during the reporting period."

2. **Opening with appreciation**: Begin documents acknowledging contributions and dedication [ESSENTIAL - CULTURAL NORM]

   *Example:* "This annual report is undoubtedly a time to reflect on the challenges and major successes of the year. Without our hard-working and truly dedicated staff members, such excellence would not have been possible."

   *Counter-example:* "This document presents organizational performance data for the fiscal year."

3. **Future-focused conclusions**: End sections with forward-looking commitments or aspirations [CRITICAL FOR MOMENTUM]

   *Example:* "With every review of our progress, we will keep in mind the purpose that is so important – enabling our organization to attract, develop and retain the best people, in order to deliver outstanding care."

   *Counter-example:* "This concludes the annual reporting requirements for the current fiscal year."

4. **Progress narrative**: Frame content as journey from past achievements through current challenges to future ambitions [IMPORTANT FOR ENGAGEMENT]

   *Example:* "Much progress was made during the last period. We want to build on those successes, and learn from the things that didn't quite achieve the results we expected... By the end of this new strategy period, we want to have made further progress."

   *Counter-example:* "The organization will implement new strategies to replace previous unsuccessful initiatives."

## Content Architecture

### Document Structure Philosophy

1. **Context setting**: Always establish the "why" before the "what" [ESSENTIAL - PURPOSE CLARITY]

   *Example:* "Understanding the case for, and the impact of, our interventions is vital, to ensure efficacy and minimize waste. Our commitment is to base the interventions we prioritise and implement on evidence gleaned from data and / or research."

   *Counter-example:* "The following interventions will be implemented according to the established timeline."

2. **Balanced reporting**: Acknowledge both successes and areas for improvement [NON-NEGOTIABLE - TRANSPARENCY]

   *Example:* "We achieved many of our targets, despite another difficult year of complex demand and staffing challenges. However, difficulties still exist for some patients in being able to access services in a timely way."

   *Counter-example:* "All performance targets were met successfully" OR "The organization failed to meet most of its objectives."

3. **Measurable outcomes**: Include specific targets, metrics, and timelines [CRITICAL FOR ACCOUNTABILITY]

   *Example:* "We aim to increase staff retention by X%... Improve patient access times to Y weeks... Increase workforce diversity representation to Z% by 2030."

   *Counter-example:* "We will work to improve performance across all relevant indicators."

4. **Interconnected themes**: Show how different areas support each other [IMPORTANT FOR SYSTEMS THINKING]

   *Example:* "Many of our objectives contribute to multiple strategic areas; for example our objective to improve our recruitment processes will contribute to our workforce capacity goals as well as to our inclusion objectives."

   *Counter-example:* "Each department will work independently to achieve its specific objectives."

### Strategic Framework Language

When discussing strategic priorities, consider these core themes common to NHS organizations:
1. **Workforce** - recruitment, retention, and development capacity
2. **Organization Design** - structural and role optimization
3. **Leadership** - development and capability building
4. **Inclusion and Belonging** - equity, diversity, and wellbeing
5. **Quality and Safety** - clinical effectiveness and patient safety
6. **System Partnership** - collaboration and integration
7. **Sustainability** - financial and environmental responsibility

### Core Enablers Framework

Always consider these cross-cutting elements in planning:
- **Employee Voice** - listening, feedback, and engagement
- **Data & Evidence** - research-informed decision making
- **Digital & Technology** - innovation and efficiency

## Stylistic Guidelines

### Sentence Structure

1. **Inclusive pronouns**: Use "we," "our," "us" to create shared ownership [ESSENTIAL - COLLABORATION IDENTITY]

   *Example:* "Our strategy ambitions are well-aligned with national NHS priorities... We have tested this approach with colleagues and stakeholders."

   *Counter-example:* "The strategy's ambitions align with national objectives... The development team tested the approach during preliminary phases."

2. **Active voice predominant**: "We will implement" rather than "will be implemented" [NON-NEGOTIABLE - AGENCY]

   *Example:* "We will increase the breadth and quality of our recruitment approaches... We will enhance our workforce development capacity."

   *Counter-example:* "Enhanced recruitment techniques will be implemented... Workforce capacity will be enhanced."

3. **Specific rather than generic**: Use specific examples and context [CRITICAL FOR PERSONALIZATION]

   *Example:* "Our communities are receiving highly effective services and achieving much better outcomes... More than X staff now work in integrated care roles."

   *Counter-example:* "The organization serves a large population and delivers care to thousands of service users daily."

4. **Varied length**: Mix short impactful statements with longer explanatory sentences [IMPORTANT FOR ENGAGEMENT]

   *Example:* "This has been a challenging year. There have been competing priorities; to maintain service quality, continue to transform services, achieve financial balance and meet key performance indicators."

   *Counter-example:* "The reporting period presented numerous challenges including service quality maintenance, transformation initiatives, and financial balance achievement."

### Paragraph Development

1. **Topic sentence with context**: Lead with the strategic significance [ESSENTIAL - CLARITY]

   *Example:* "A vital part of keeping the best people is looking after them well, creating and sustaining an environment in which they can thrive, and providing support in times where health & wellbeing is at risk or compromised."

   *Counter-example:* "Employee wellbeing programs have been implemented."

2. **Supporting evidence**: Include data, examples, or stakeholder quotes [NON-NEGOTIABLE - CREDIBILITY]

   *Example:* "Substantial health and wellbeing gains have been made. With an improved, increasingly tailored wellbeing offer and plans to develop further support, we are in a strong position to support the long term, sustainable recovery of our workforce."

   *Counter-example:* "Various wellbeing initiatives are in place and continue to be developed."

3. **Connection to vision**: Link back to organizational purpose [CRITICAL FOR ALIGNMENT]

   *Example:* "All of this feeds into our core commitment to attract, develop and keep the best people, in order to deliver outstanding care. This is our guiding principle and ultimate aim."

   *Counter-example:* "These initiatives support the organization's strategic objectives."

4. **Forward momentum**: End with next steps or future aspirations [IMPORTANT FOR CONTINUITY]

   *Example:* "Our focus is on measuring outcomes more than inputs; although some input measures are important to include."

   *Counter-example:* "Current measurement approaches will continue to be used."

### Data Presentation

1. **Human impact**: Connect statistics to patient and staff experience [ESSENTIAL - PURPOSE]

   *Example:* "More than X people per year able to return home following care, avoiding longer-term residential placement. Y% decrease in readmission rates after receiving home-based support."

   *Counter-example:* "Residential placement avoidance: X per year. Readmission reduction: Y%."

2. **Contextual framing**: Explain what numbers mean for people and services [NON-NEGOTIABLE - UNDERSTANDING]

   *Example:* "Z people avoiding a hospital admission each year. This represents a significant reduction in unnecessary hospitalization and improved community-based care."

   *Counter-example:* "Key performance indicators: X, Y%, Z."

3. **Honest assessment**: Acknowledge both achievements and shortfalls [CRITICAL FOR TRUST]

   *Example:* "We are pleased to have achieved significant progress in sustainability initiatives... Although we still have more work to do, the foundation we have created will enable continued improvement."

   *Counter-example:* "All targets were met successfully" OR "Initiatives continue to show mixed results."

4. **Trend awareness**: Compare to previous periods and external benchmarks [IMPORTANT FOR CONTEXT]

   *Example:* "We are proud to see our continued focus on workforce diversity making an impact. Colleagues from diverse backgrounds now represent X% of our workforce... we are working towards Y% by Z date."

   *Counter-example:* "Current diversity representation stands at X%."

## Sector-Specific Language

### NHS and Healthcare Terms

- Use full terms on first reference, then abbreviations (e.g., "Integrated Care System (ICS)")

  *Example:* "The establishment of new Integrated Care Boards and partnerships brings further opportunities for collaboration... We are central to system-wide objectives including leadership development and talent management."

  *Counter-example:* "ICS and partnership initiatives will enhance collaboration."

- Explain technical concepts in accessible language

  *Example:* "We have implemented the Patient Safety Incident Response Framework (PSIRF) this year. The PSIRF challenges us to think and respond differently when a patient safety incident occurs. It's a learning and improvement framework that looks at how a system and a culture can support improvement."

  *Counter-example:* "PSIRF implementation continues according to national mandates."

- Reference relevant NHS frameworks (NHS People Plan, Long Term Plan, etc.)

  *Example:* "Our strategy ambitions are well-aligned with the NHS People Promise and the NHS People Plan, which seek for the NHS to have more staff, working differently, in a compassionate and inclusive culture."

  *Counter-example:* "Organizational objectives align with national requirements."

- Include appropriate regulatory and quality frameworks (CQC, NICE, etc.)

  *Example:* "Our recent regulatory assessment noted our commitment to quality improvement. We measure our effectiveness against NICE Guidance and other relevant clinical standards."

  *Counter-example:* "Recent regulatory assessments have been satisfactory."

### Healthcare Service Focus

- Emphasize person-centered, community-based care delivery

  *Example:* "Care is always provided in, or as near to, the person's own home as possible... We have reaffirmed our commitment to working together on a community-centered agenda."

  *Counter-example:* "Services are delivered through established clinical pathways."

- Highlight prevention and early intervention

  *Example:* "Preventive services to avoid further care and treatment, particularly emergency admissions, that could be addressed sooner and before they become serious."

  *Counter-example:* "Treatment protocols are implemented according to clinical guidelines."

- Discuss integration across health and social care

  *Example:* "We work in partnership with health, social care, local government and the third sector... By working in true partnership, we can deliver better integrated models within sustainable resources."

  *Counter-example:* "The organization maintains relationships with various partner agencies."

- Reference the communities and geographies served

  *Example:* "We serve a diverse population across multiple communities... across the entire service area and beyond through specialist services."

  *Counter-example:* "Services are provided across the regional catchment area."

### Quality and Safety Language

- **"Person-centered care"** - individual focus and dignity
- **"Safe, effective, caring, responsive, well-led"** - quality domains
- **"Outstanding care"** - quality aspiration
- **"Continuous improvement"** - learning culture

## Emotional Resonance

### Appreciation and Recognition

1. **Regularly acknowledge colleague dedication and expertise** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - CORE VALUE]

   *Example:* "During the year many individuals, teams and services received recognition for their achievements... We regularly celebrate colleague achievements and innovations."

   *Counter-example:* "Staff recognition programs are in place."

2. **Celebrate specific achievements and innovations** [ESSENTIAL - MOTIVATION]

   *Example:* "Our organization has been instrumental in innovative approaches. Examples include creative solutions that have been recognized regionally and nationally."

   *Counter-example:* "Various innovative approaches have been implemented."

3. **Recognize partnership contributions** [CRITICAL FOR RELATIONSHIPS]

   *Example:* "We have worked successfully with partner organizations and communities to improve outcomes... partnerships that have proven to be transformative."

   *Counter-example:* "Partnerships continue to operate effectively."

4. **Thank stakeholders and service users for feedback** [IMPORTANT FOR ENGAGEMENT]

   *Example:* "We welcome all forms of feedback as an opportunity to improve. It can be difficult to speak up when things go wrong but to learn from and develop services, we must be ready to listen."

   *Counter-example:* "Feedback mechanisms are maintained according to policy requirements."

### Challenge Acknowledgment

1. **Balance realism with optimism** [ESSENTIAL - CREDIBILITY AND HOPE]

   *Example:* "We achieved many of our targets, despite a challenging year of complex demand and staffing pressures. However, difficulties still exist for some patients. Despite this, most of our patients received timely care."

   *Counter-example:* "All challenges have been successfully overcome" OR "Multiple system failures continue to impact performance."

2. **Frame difficulties as opportunities for learning and growth** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - IMPROVEMENT CULTURE]

   *Example:* "Any large organizational change can be a challenge. To address this as positively as possible with frontline colleagues, we designed collaborative improvement approaches that prioritized colleague ideas for change."

   *Counter-example:* "Budget constraints have created significant organizational challenges."

3. **Emphasize collective responsibility for solutions** [CRITICAL FOR EMPOWERMENT]

   *Example:* "Nevertheless, this brings enormous opportunities for us, and we are confident that, through our values and behaviors, we can build on the strong foundations we have established."

   *Counter-example:* "Management will determine appropriate responses to current challenges."

4. **Acknowledge external pressures while maintaining agency** [IMPORTANT FOR CONTEXT]

   *Example:* "We continue to work in a changing environment with competing priorities. A key focus will be on supporting and enabling our organization in adapting to these challenges."

   *Counter-example:* "External factors have disrupted organizational plans."

### Inspirational Elements

1. **Connect work to meaningful patient outcomes** [ESSENTIAL - CORE PURPOSE]

   *Example:* "Our services are improving outcomes for people and communities. Most importantly, residents are receiving highly effective services and achieving much better results."

   *Counter-example:* "Operational efficiencies have been achieved across multiple service lines."

2. **Reinforce organizational purpose and values** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - IDENTITY]

   *Example:* "We uphold our values through our behaviors and approach to every interaction with patients, colleagues and partners."

   *Counter-example:* "Organizational values guide operational decisions."

3. **Emphasize professional development and career growth** [CRITICAL FOR RETENTION]

   *Example:* "Colleagues complete development programs with outcomes including growth in confidence, leadership ability, and career progression... 'I now recognize that I have lots to offer and opportunities for progression.'"

   *Counter-example:* "Training programs are available for staff development."

4. **Highlight innovation and transformation potential** [IMPORTANT FOR FUTURE]

   *Example:* "Innovation has been high on our agenda. Innovations include new approaches to clinical and operational support."

   *Counter-example:* "Technology upgrades have been implemented."

## Inclusive Communication

### Equity and Diversity

1. **Emphasize commitment to equity and anti-racism** [ESSENTIAL - ORGANIZATIONAL COMMITMENT]

   *Example:* "As an organization committed to equity and anti-racism, we will consider how we address racism and discrimination in all our processes... We deliver cultural competency training to support inclusive practice."

   *Counter-example:* "Equality training is provided to meet compliance requirements."

2. **Acknowledge "lived experience" and "diverse perspectives"** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - AUTHENTIC INCLUSION]

   *Example:* "We work with our staff networks and groups to better understand and incorporate their lived experience and diverse needs into our approaches and policies."

   *Counter-example:* "Staff input is gathered through established channels."

3. **Reference "protected characteristics" when discussing equality** [CRITICAL FOR LEGAL/ETHICAL COMPLIANCE]

   *Example:* "We will identify and tackle areas of disparity in employee experience by protected characteristic, focusing on race, disability and sexual orientation."

   *Counter-example:* "Equality measures apply to all staff groups."

4. **Use inclusive and respectful terminology** [IMPORTANT FOR SPECIFICITY]

   *Example:* "Our commitment to equity centers on identifying systemic barriers and acting to address them... Colleagues from diverse backgrounds bring valuable perspectives to our work."

   *Counter-example:* "Diversity initiatives address various demographic categories."

### Accessibility

1. **Write in plain English with clear, jargon-free explanations** [ESSENTIAL - UNIVERSAL ACCESS]

   *Example:* "We make feedback easy for everyone including people whose first language is not English, people who use different communication methods, and people with different accessibility needs."

   *Counter-example:* "Communication modalities are optimized for diverse demographic requirements."

2. **Reference "reasonable adjustments" and "accessible information"** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - LEGAL DUTY]

   *Example:* "We provide information in formats and languages that meet different needs, including reasonable adjustments for people with disabilities."

   *Counter-example:* "Communication support is provided according to policy requirements."

3. **Consider multiple communication needs and preferences** [CRITICAL FOR INCLUSION]

   *Example:* "We provide multiple ways to access information including different languages, audio formats, and easy-read versions."

   *Counter-example:* "Digital accessibility features have been implemented."

4. **Use inclusive examples that reflect community diversity** [IMPORTANT FOR REPRESENTATION]

   *Example:* "We want to make sure there is equity in everything we do... our long-term equity plan moves us 'from intent to action'... safe spaces for colleagues from diverse backgrounds to connect and support each other."

   *Counter-example:* "Equity measures are applied across all patient groups."

### Staff Experience

1. **Emphasize psychological safety and speaking up culture** [ESSENTIAL - SAFETY AND TRUST]

   *Example:* "We must have support in place for patients and colleagues involved in patient safety incidents. We need to create a safe and 'just' culture where people feel confident to speak up when things don't go as planned."

   *Counter-example:* "Incident reporting procedures are maintained according to requirements."

2. **Acknowledge different professional backgrounds and roles** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - INCLUSIVE RECOGNITION]

   *Example:* "Dedication in clinical, corporate and support roles has maintained the highest standard of service... Participants from diverse professional backgrounds have contributed to improvement work."

   *Counter-example:* "All staff members contribute to organizational objectives."

3. **Reference various career stages and development needs** [CRITICAL FOR GROWTH]

   *Example:* "We provide development opportunities for emerging leaders as well as more experienced colleagues... mentoring schemes and coaching support different career stages."

   *Counter-example:* "Professional development opportunities are available at all levels."

4. **Consider different working patterns and life circumstances** [IMPORTANT FOR RETENTION]

   *Example:* "We attract and retain more staff by enabling flexible working options that meet individual and service needs... new working approaches that better meet organizational and employee requirements."

   *Counter-example:* "Flexible working arrangements are implemented according to policy."

## Formatting and Language Standards

### 1. **Bold Text Usage** [ESSENTIAL - DOCUMENT CLARITY]

   **Correct Usage**: Bold text should ONLY be used for headings and subheadings to maintain document hierarchy and readability.

   *Example:* "### Strategic Direction" or "#### Key Success Factors"

   *Counter-example:* Using bold within paragraph text like "We will achieve **outstanding results** through **collaborative working**"

### 2. **UK English Spelling** [NON-NEGOTIABLE - ORGANIZATIONAL STANDARD]

   **Required Standards**: All documents must use UK English spelling conventions consistently throughout.

   *Examples:*
   - "organisation" not "organization"
   - "realise" not "realize"
   - "colour" not "color"
   - "centre" not "center"
   - "programme" not "program"
   - "recognised" not "recognized"

   *Counter-examples:* Any use of American English spellings

### 3. **Reference Numbering and Citation** [CRITICAL FOR CREDIBILITY]

   **In-text Citations**: Use numbered references in square brackets linked to a comprehensive reference list.

   *Example:* "This aligns with NHS Long Term Workforce Plan objectives [1] and supports the health system's transformation agenda [2]."

   *Counter-example:* "This aligns with NHS Long Term Workforce Plan objectives (NHS England, p.72)"

   **Reference List**: Include complete reference list at document end with correct numbering.

   *Example:*
   ```
   ## References

   [1] NHS England. NHS Long Term Workforce Plan. London: NHS England; 2023.
   [2] Department of Health and Social Care. Health System Policy. London: DHSC; 2024.
   [3] Your Organization. Annual Report and Accounts 2024-25. [Location]: Your Organization; 2025.
   ```

   *Counter-example:* No reference list or inconsistent numbering

## Preserving References During Styling

When applying this style guide to reformat an analysis document (gap analysis, evidence synthesis, research review, etc.):

### Citation Preservation Rule [NON-NEGOTIABLE]

All inline citations from the original analysis document MUST be preserved during reformatting. Do NOT remove, consolidate, or lose any citations.

### Two Acceptable Approaches

**Option A: Keep Inline Citations (Simplest)**
- Preserve all [Source: filename] citations inline within the text as they appear
- No conversion needed
- Example: "This is supported by evidence [Source: NHS England Productivity.md]"

**Option B: Convert to Numbered References (If document format requires it)**
- Extract ALL citations from original BEFORE reformatting begins
- Convert EVERY [Source: filename] to numbered brackets [1], [2], etc.
- Create comprehensive reference list at document end matching every number
- MUST be exhaustive - every citation number referenced must have corresponding entry
- Example: "This is supported by evidence [1]" → Reference list includes [1]

### Citation Validation - Before Publishing

- [ ] Count total citations in original document
- [ ] Count total citations in reformatted document
- [ ] Citation counts match exactly
- [ ] No orphaned citations (e.g., reference to [1] with no [1] in reference list)
- [ ] If using numbered format, reference list is complete and sequential

## Customization for Your Organization

This guide has been written to be applicable to all NHS organizations. To customize for your specific trust, ICB, or health system:

1. Replace generic references with your organization's specific values and priorities
2. Use your organization's specific terminology and strategic frameworks
3. Update examples to reflect your care model and service areas
4. Include your organization's specific initiatives and programs
5. Reference your organization's published strategies and reports

The core principles of community-centered, warmly professional, evidence-based communication apply universally across the NHS.
