We are the NHS West Yorkshire

Integrated Care Board, also called the

ICB. ICBs are a new type of NHS

organisation that was set up by the

government in July 2022.


Our ICB has five local places which are

Bradford District and Craven, Calderdale,

Kirklees, Leeds, and Wakefield District.


We are made up of doctors, nurses and

professionals from lots of different

backgrounds.


Our job is to plan, buy and look after most

of the health services that you and your

family need.


We also help to make sure that local

health and care services work together.


This is so that the people of West

Yorkshire get the best possible care, no

matter who they are or where they live.


Working together also means we can

share our skills, resources and money

and give a better service to everyone.


We want to give you a picture of some of

the things we did between April 2024 and

March 2025.


In 2022, we worked together to develop a

new five-year plan.


The plan sets out how we will work

together to:


improve the health of children and


young people


support people to stay well and


independent


help people with conditions that could


be prevented


support people with long-term


conditions or mental health issues


care for people with lots of different


needs especially as people are living

longer


share our resources so people can get


the care they need as quickly as

possible.


You can find out more about our plan on

our website

We understand how important it is for

people to be able to get the right

healthcare when they need it.


During 2024-25, there were about

1,474,368 GP appointments every month

in West Yorkshire.


We also made more appointments

available for people needing an NHS

dentist.


We have continued to work with doctors,

nurses and other health professionals in

the community.


This means that people can be cared for

closer to home and not have to go to

hospital.


We continued to help people manage

long-term conditions like diabetes, high

blood pressure, heart or lung disease.


We promoted healthier living messages

so people can live good lives. This

included visiting sports events and

community venues to test people’s blood

pressure and talk to them about their

health.


We gave extra money to mental health

services so that more people can get help

when they need it.


We also made sure that people with

learning disabilities were cared for and

were part of our priority groups.


We continued to work with our local

pharmacies so they can give health

advice as well as medicines.


Nearly all pharmacies in West Yorkshire

are signed up to Pharmacy First.


This means that people can go to a

pharmacy to get medicines for lots of

common illnesses or if they need an

urgent repeat medicine.


Health and care services all over the

country are still recovering from the

impact of Covid-19.


More people are needing services, we

have less money and there are lots of

other challenges.


But we have still provided more

healthcare this year than last year.


Waits of over one year for a non-urgent

operation have more than halved during

the year 2024 to 2025.


We also made progress on cutting waiting

times for people needing planned

operations or cancer treatment.


We need to do more to make sure people

are able to leave hospital more quickly,

with the right support in place.


We also need to keep working to cut

down the time people wait for planned

operations, and autism and ADHD

assessments and services.


Please remember that services are busy,

but your NHS is here for you.


If you feel poorly or are worried about

your health, please get advice from your

GP practice or pharmacy.


If you feel ill but it isn’t an emergency,

visit http://www.111.nhs.uk or call **111** .


Trained medical staff will be able to

help you, any time of day or night, any

day of the year.


In an emergency situation call **999** .
