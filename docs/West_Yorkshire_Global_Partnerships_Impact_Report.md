**West Yorkshire Global Partnerships Impact Report  /2**


#### **Executive summary**

West Yorkshire Health and Care Partnership (WYHCP) participates in global partnerships
as part of its commitment to improving health equity and outcomes, and to meet its aim of
being a good global citizen. As a system of sanctuary, WYHCP recognises its responsibility
to both local and international communities, valuing global connections that improve health
and care outcomes. Engaging in global partnerships supports West Yorkshire’s strategic
priorities by strengthening services, empowering staff, and promoting innovation and
inclusion.

The core purpose of global partnerships is to collaborate across organisational and
international boundaries to improve health and wellbeing. By working in partnership, the
West Yorkshire Global Partnerships Team, WYHCP organisations, NHS England and
Global Partners address shared challenges and facilitate learning exchanges that benefit
both systems. Partnerships are grounded in equity and mutual benefit, ensuring all partners
gain from shared expertise and experience.

The approach taken is collaborative and flexible. Partnerships are developed through
strategic planning to identify joint priorities. Activities undertaken together include virtual
knowledge exchange, Observerships in West Yorkshire, Global Fellowships abroad, and
ethical international recruitment. These activities are coordinated by the Global
Partnerships Team, which provides strategic oversight, supports delivery, and ensures
alignment with WYHCP’s values and goals.

The benefits of global partnerships are significant and wide-reaching. Staff across West
Yorkshire gain cultural competence, leadership development, and improved wellbeing,
enhancing retention and service quality. Reciprocal innovation allows for the adoption of
effective practices from Global Partners, while international recruitment helps maintain safe
staffing through ethical pathways. Global partnerships promote equity by recognising and
valuing internationally educated colleagues.

Looking forward, the future of West Yorkshire’s global partnerships includes a shift towards
more strategic commissioning, further aligning partnerships with population health needs
and system priorities. There are ambitions to expand commercial global health activities,
generating revenue to reinvest in local services. As partnerships mature, the focus will be
on building sustainable models that embed global engagement across the system, ensuring
that West Yorkshire continues to lead in integrating global health into local care.

##### **Way of working**

West Yorkshire takes a partnership approach to working globally, collaborating across
organisational and international boundaries to improve the health and wellbeing of people in
West Yorkshire. The Global Partnerships Team works with West Yorkshire Health and Care
Partnership (WYHCP) organisations to deliver for our communities. This innovative
approach supports not only NHS organisations but also local government, VCSE (voluntary,
community, and social enterprise), educational and research organisations, and social care
to benefit from global partnerships. Working as a region, with strong relationships to
organisations with shared values supports West Yorkshire to work with Global Partners to
mutually strengthen their health and care systems. WYHCP is at the cutting edge nationally
in this whole-system approach through its exemplar partnership with NHSE.

The Global Partnerships Team is based in the West Yorkshire ICB (Integrated Care Board).
It coordinates, supports, and has strategic oversight of partnerships between WYHCP
organisations, Global Partner governments and organisations, charities and funders, and
NHS England. The Global Partnerships Team commissions WYHCP organisations and
supports them in delivery. West Yorkshire’s global partnerships have all come about through
different means, including those which evolved from smaller-scale collaborations to those
identified by the UK government and offered nationally by NHS England. The Global
Partnerships Team leads on projects spanning WYHCP organisations, including Global
Fellowships. The Global Partnerships Team also support West Yorkshire as a region to take
up wider opportunities for international collaboration and connection, for example by
participating and supporting international trade missions and the building of wider
relationships for West Yorkshire and Global Partners.

West Yorkshire’s Global Partnerships Team are partnered with and supported by the NHS
England Global Health Unit (GHU). The GHU strategically support a range of partnerships
across England, including its unique partnership with West Yorkshire. They are
commissioned by UK government (including FCDO and DHSC) to scope, build, and
commission global partnerships. Colleagues from the NHSE GHU support some West
Yorkshire global partnerships with strategy and project management.

WYHCP organisations deliver global partnership activities, including virtual learning, ethical
international recruitment, and Observerships. The Global Partnerships Team offers
guidance and expertise, building capacity to independently deliver global partnerships.

Working collaboratively means each partnership is unique in organisation and delivery.
Strategy setting is often achieved through a Global Star Workforce Transformation
Workshop, led by the GHU on behalf of partners. These structured sessions support

#### **Global learning**
##### **Virtual knowledge exchange**

Virtual learning opportunities are a core part of the Global Partnerships offer. Online
platforms offer a cost effective, flexible, and accessible way for staff from West Yorkshire
and partner countries to access **learning** . Working with Global Partners develops cultural
sensitivity and intercultural communication skills, **promoting equity** . Sharing ways of
working between partners supports **reciprocal innovation** for mutual health system
strengthening.

At its simplest, virtual knowledge exchange can involve sharing resources (including
policies, procedures, and care pathways) between individuals or teams for adaption and
adoption. Communities of Practice (CoPs) hosted online support collaborative discussions
and shared problem solving. LTHT are establishing a CoP with Saint Vincent and the
Grenadines to connect clinicians interested and involved in infection prevention and control,
supporting **reciprocal innovation** and **learning** . Mid Yorks and OET (an English language
learning specialist company) are establishing a CoP to support international recruits to
develop their English language skills. This supports integration which is important for both
**wellbeing** and staff **retention** .

Virtual Clinical Grand Rounds (VCGRs) involve clinicians from West Yorkshire and a Global
Partner regularly coming together to share and discuss case studies. VGRs are a tested
model leveraging the Grand Round method of healthcare education and applying it to
[global work. VGRs are the first step in the Uganda-UK SCALE programme, which build into](https://uukha.org/scale/)
further partnership development including bi-directional people exchange through
Fellowships and Observerships.

West Yorkshire Global Partnerships are using VGRs to support the development of new
partnerships by deploying a tested and effective model. Using virtual learning at an early
stage allows stakeholders to collaborate and build relationships by working together to
deliver training. To support stakeholders in this West Yorkshire have established an online
Global Partnerships CoP. This aims to **connect the system** by bringing colleagues involved
in the delivery of virtual knowledge exchange together for mutual learning and support,
thereby **empowering providers** to take ownership of the partnership and effectively realise
the benefits working in global partnerships.

##### **West Yorkshire Observerships**

Observerships see colleagues from Global
Partner countries spending one to two months
in West Yorkshire to support their learning and
development. Observers are expected to
complete a quality improvement project in their
home country following the Observership to
implement changes based on their learning in
West Yorkshire. LTHT has hosted two cohorts

of Observers from Saint Vincent and the
Grenadines, with further cohorts from SVG and
Kerala planned. SVG Observers in training, Leeds (2022)

Observerships strategically focus on defined priorities for Global Partners to support health
system strengthening, often as an outcome from a collaborative STAR Workshop. Global
Partners identify Observership candidates, sharing their details and learning interests with
the West Yorkshire host organisation who plan their visit to support their development.

When in West Yorkshire, Observers are supported by mentors, experienced colleagues
who arrange opportunities for them to learn in their chosen clinical area. To effectively
support Observers, mentor work with them to **learn** about health and care the Global
Partner country. This **promotes equity** by supporting mentors to develop knowledge and
understanding of international health practices and beliefs, and their cultural basis.
Observers are also supported pastorally through regular check-ins, support adjusting to
West Yorkshire and their host institution, and through provision of accommodation and a
stipend.

Observers attend training sessions individually in their area of interest and general courses
on wider skills. They spend time in clinical areas shadowing West Yorkshire staff to learn
about practices and identify quality improvement project topics for their return. Mentors and
other staff involved work with Observers to develop their quality improvement projects.
Differences between resources and healthcare practices between host and Global Partner
countries mean staff must adapt and challenge their thinking by applying it to new
situations. This creates opportunities for **reciprocal innovation** . Supporting staff to use and
value their professional skills in a new way to help others enhances **wellbeing** .

On return, Observers complete their quality improvement project and share learning with
colleagues, positive effects which endure beyond the end of Observers’ time in West
Yorkshire. Some Observers also remain in contact with their mentors; building a crosscultural friendship supports **wellbeing** and **promotes equity**, while sharing knowledge and
practices offers further opportunities for **reciprocal innovation** and **learning** .

In addition to the model described above, the Global Partnerships Team supports other
learning exchange visits. In 2024 three Ugandan clinician were hosted in West Yorkshire
where they attended training and visited clinical areas to gain exposure. Future learning
visits will use a range of models, developed and adapted to achieve agreed benefits for
Global Partners and West Yorkshire.

#### **Ethical international recruitment**

##### **Ethical migratory pathways**

Working in collaboration with providers, NHS England, and Global Partners, the West
Yorkshire Global Partnerships Team has created several effective ethical migratory
pathways (EMPs). These EMPs have recruited Allied Health Professionals (radiographers),
Nurses (mental health, acute, social care, senior clinical advisors), and Medics (medical
oncology) to roles in, acute and mental health Trusts, social care, and Yorkshire Ambulance
Service.

EMPs are designed to meet the needs of providers, who value international recruitment for
**supporting safe staffing** and meeting the needs of the communities they serve. The UK
Government is currently seeking to reduce reliance on international recruitment in health
and social care; however, current projections indicate international recruitment will continue
to be required through any transitional period.

Partners work together throughout the design and delivery of EMPs. providers commission
the West Yorkshire Global Partnerships Team to support them in recruiting colleagues
internationally. NHS England scope international recruitment options, drawing on their
significant experience in this area. Once options are identified Global Partners are engaged
with to support recruitment by identifying and screening candidates. Providers shortlist,
interview, and recruit candidates. The Global Partnerships Team supports providers in
navigating this process and helps ensure they offer effective pastoral support, tailored to
recruits’ needs.

Recruiting from Global Partner countries has three significant benefits. First, it ensures
Global Partners can effectively manage the international migration of their health and care
professionals, which supports them and ensures pathways are sustainable. Second,
working together in global partnerships allows West Yorkshire to influence staff health and
care training before arrival, ensuring staff have the right skills and pathways are
sustainable. Third, Global Partner engagement in the recruitment process provides access
to recruitment services at zero cost, making recruitment pathways more cost efficient and
thereby **improving the financial position** .

EMPs focus on the person and their journey. Moving to a new country is a big undertaking;
West Yorkshire has a responsibility to look after the wellbeing of staff joining them in serving
their communities. The Global Partnerships Team works with providers, supporting them to
[deliver high quality pastoral support to recruits. Following NHS best practice for](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/international-recruitment-toolkit)
[international recruitment promotes](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/international-recruitment-toolkit) **wellbeing,** which in turn **supports retention** .

All EMPs are evaluated to identify enablers and learn lessons to support future international
recruitment. This has created a significant body of institutional knowledge which supports
effective and efficient recruitment pathway design and delivery. Best practice is shared to
**empower providers** to deliver high quality pastoral support, supporting **wellbeing** and
**promoting equity** between recruitment offers for internationally recruited colleagues.
Following best practice can support effective delivery on investments associated with
international recruitment, which **improves the financial position** and ensure targets are
achieved. The expertise of the Global Partnerships Team leads to effective recruitment and
provides access to the benefits of working with Global Partners to **support safe staffing.**

relationships to blossom by harnessing their engagement. The Global Partnerships Team
uses the ‘Global Citizens Community’ mailing list and newsletter to connect with passionate
people and support their engagement.

**Communicating and engaging**
Supporting colleagues to access the benefits of global partnerships requires strong comms
and engagement. The Global Partnerships Team has developed a strategy and plan to
guide work and ensure colleagues are engaged and informed on global partnerships
activities.

##### **Overcoming challenges**

**Finances**
Financing global partnerships work can be challenging at time when budgets are under
strain in the health and care system. The Global Partnerships Team works to overcome this
challenge by:

Using low-cost delivery methods, for example hosting training online.

Sourcing external funding for global partnerships (charities, NHSE, government).

Offering volunteer opportunities.

Looking to commercialise offering to generate revenue and fund global partnerships

work.

Strong relationships with Global Partners are essential. The Global Partnerships Team uses

##### **Strategically commissioning global partnership**

The Global Partnerships Team secures funds for partnerships, sharing these with providers
and commissioning them to complete partnership activities. This supports providers to
achieve the benefits associated with global partnerships, helping them meet population
health needs and develop their workforce.

This approach can be further developed using a strategic commissioning model. This is a
more strategic approach to connecting providers with Global Partner countries, informed by
population health needs and priorities for organisational development.

To achieve the three big shifts towards community-based care, prevention, and greater use
of digital, West Yorkshire can strategically commission partnerships which support learning
[and reciprocal innovation in these areas. The Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board in](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-023-10084-8)
[Wales drew on a Community Health Worker model from Brazil to transform community](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-023-10084-8)
[services, significantly increasing immunisation and cancer screening uptake while reducing](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-023-10084-8)
GP appointments. Thinking strategically about opportunities for reciprocal innovation in
West Yorkshire partnerships can support population health improvement and help achieve
the three big shifts.

Opportunities for reciprocal innovation, increased quality improvement (QI) skills, and
leadership development are benefits of Global Fellowships. Developing the model for
Fellowship delivery to include protected time post-Fellowship for Fellows to share what they
learned and complete a QI project in West Yorkshire uses this insight to secure further
benefits for West Yorkshire.

[A recent review by Global Health Partnerships (formerly THET) suggested the NHS should](https://www.globalhealthpartnerships.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Health-worker-wellbeing-and-global-engagement-UPDATED.pdf)
[integrate global health into its wellbeing strategy. West Yorkshire evaluation insight confirms](https://www.globalhealthpartnerships.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Health-worker-wellbeing-and-global-engagement-UPDATED.pdf)
global health involvement supports staff wellbeing. Integrating global participation into West
Yorkshire’s wellbeing offer uses evidence to guide how partnerships are commissioned.

##### **Achieving West Yorkshire’s international commercial potential**

The Right Honourable Wes Streeting MP, Secretary of State for Health and Social Care
announced the NHS and social care are “engines of economic growth”. DHSC has asked
NHSE to support its wealth creation ambitions by identifying unmet revenue generation
potential, including through commercial global partnerships. West Yorkshire can align with
this national policy direction by following the lead of other organisations and offering new
global partnerships on a commercial basis, paid for by a partner country. This would allow
West Yorkshire to access the benefits of global health and care partnerships while
generating revenue from contracts which can be invested in supporting communities in
West Yorkshire.

The NHS brand is globally renowned, and the NHS has a unique selling point as the world’s
oldest universal health coverage system. There is a proven global demand for the learning
and expertise of the NHS, and NHS organisations are already benefitting from their
engagement in international commercial activities.

West Yorkshire stakeholders are working in the international commercial space. They
represent opportunities for collaboration and learning in the development of this work, a
route to access markets, and the potential to develop an integrated commercial strategy for
health and care across West Yorkshire. These include Health Innovation Yorkshire and
Humber, West Yorkshire Combined Authority, and West Yorkshire members of the NHS
Confederation International Special Interest Group. Additionally, there is a well-developed
infrastructure to support NHS organisations to participate in international commercial
activities working in a range of ways. Developing an approach to international commercial
partnerships in West Yorkshire should involve building relationships with these stakeholders
in the short- and medium-term, before looking to involve a wider range of organisations as
the strategy and approach develops into the long-term.

Achieving West Yorkshire’s international commercial potential means working in new ways
for the organisation. This carries risks which need to be mitigated, including risks to
reputation, finances, and quality. By working gradually towards this vision and building in
appropriate review points an approach to international commercial activities can be
developed to safely take advantage of these benefits.

NHS Trusts and Hospitals throughout England already engage in international commercial
activities. Organisations offer a range of services, most frequently consultancy (professional
and clinical), and education and training. Commercial products provided are often
intangibles such as policies, procedures, QA, audits, oversight, consulting, brand
management, intellectual property. These demand business and management skillsets
which are typically easier to source than direct clinical staff and carry less risk. Many
international commercial offers can be delivered from the UK and do not require substantial
overseas presence. Turnover for a few organisations is several £million. For most it is
relatively small – c. <£1million and as little as a few thousand. Turnover varies based on
services offered, how developed the commercial offering is, and the extent to which
commercial work is prioritised.

West Yorkshire can begin working towards achieving its international commercial potential,
in alignment with government policy and strategy, by working iteratively and progressively
to design and deliver partnerships and programmes that generate revenue and realise the
benefits associated with global health and care partnerships.

##### **Vision for achieving West Yorkshire’s international commercial potential**

**Short-term potential (0-9 months)**
WYICB are realising short-term low risk opportunities to sell existing products. They are
developing relationships with key stakeholders and working towards future partnerships.

Existing resources and expertise in the Global Partnerships Team is used to deliver
opportunities and provide proof of concept. Processes to cost, administer, and govern
international commercial activities are in development. Work reports to the Global
Partnerships Board.

WYICB is generating modest revenue from the sale of existing products. There is minimal
upfront investment. Some profits are reinvested to fund the growth of international
commercial activities and provide a financial cushion to mitigate future risk.


**Looking beyond the short-term potential there is scope for international commercial**
**activities to grow and develop, subject to appropriate governance arrangements and**
**alignment to wider strategic priorities for all partners.**


**Medium term**

West Yorkshire organisations are entering partnerships using a tested model for delivering
global partnerships. Organisations are supported to identify existing products to sell.
Consultancy services start including contracts to support implementation.

International commercial expertise is becoming well developed. A Steering Group supports
the delivery of commercial partnerships and associated strategy development.
International commercial revenue is growing, profits continue to be reinvested to support
further expansion. WYHCP organisations who enter partnerships are using some profits to
improve health and care outcomes and experiences. Profitability gradually increases as
costing and delivery improves. Learning from partnerships is positively impacting on
efficiency and service delivery. There is a positive impact on wellbeing and retention.

**Long term**
A range of West Yorkshire organisations have international commercial partnerships. New
opportunities continue to be identified. West Yorkshire is working towards an integrated
international commercial strategy. As West Yorkshire’s reputation has grown, more
profitable licencing and branding opportunities can be explored. Opportunities for research
and development are emerging through longer standing partnerships.

Stakeholder engagement in the medium-term has facilitated the creation of a mutually
agreed approach to governance and risk management. Suitable corporate structures are
being developed to manage commercial contracts and revenue.

Operations show strong profitability, as previous experience has improved delivery. The
number of contracts managed is stabilising so profits no longer need to be reinvested at the
same rate to support growth. Profits are invested to support West Yorkshire communities.
